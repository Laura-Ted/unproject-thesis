# 2026-09-15 - WP6, the filter measured against a truth it finally has

WP6 of ADR-0005. The root filter has been scored on real footage for the
apparent depth velocity it removes - 4.66 m/s to 1.28 on the crowd clip - and
the ADR's rule is that this is smoothness, never accuracy, because the
footage has no truth. This commit gives the filter a truth: a deterministic
suite of synthetic root trajectories, and the same `TrackSmoother` the
analysis uses, measured for what a filter is measured for.

## What was built

`unproject.rq3.characterise`, and `unproject filter-characterise`:

- **signals**, from seed 20260915: constant with Gaussian noise at 3 % of the
  distance (the measured jitter fraction), a 1 m step, a 1 m/s ramp, and
  sinusoids at 0.1–2 Hz below the cadence's Nyquist limit; for the depth
  axis at 2.5, 10 and 25 samples/s and at 2, 8, 20 and 40 m; for the lateral
  axis at the reference 8 m, since it does not follow range;
- **metrics**: RMS before and after and their ratio; t90, settling into a
  10 % band, overshoot; ramp lag in metres and seconds; gain in dB and phase
  delay from a sine-and-cosine fit after the first period;
- **outputs**: JSON, CSV, an attenuation-per-frequency figure and a step-
  response figure, all regenerating byte for byte;
- **parity**: `tests/fixtures/one_euro_signals.json`, generated by
  `parity_fixture` - a noisy step in depth and a noisy sine sideways at 2.5
  and 25 samples/s and at 2 and 40 m, 160 samples - checked here to be what
  the code generates and to be reproduced by the Python filter, and in
  `bodies.test.ts` to be reproduced by the viewer's. It joins `one_euro.json`,
  which covered the defaults at one cadence.

`docs/experiments/filter-characterisation.md` is the frozen protocol.

## What the suite says, at the reference

At 8 m and 10 samples/s, the viewer's defaults: noise RMS 0.240 m → 0.108 m
(×0.45); a 1 m step reaches 90 % after 0.7 s with no overshoot; a 1 m/s walk
is followed 0.5 s behind; motion at 0.25 Hz passes at −0.9 dB delayed 0.24 s,
at 1 Hz at −6.7 dB delayed 0.13 s. At 40 m the same step takes 3.2 s and the
walker is 1.5 s behind; at 2 m, 0.3 s and 0.3 s. The trade-off the README
describes in words - tighter at range, and the price is delay - is now a
table, and the tests pin its direction: attenuation grows with distance, so
do t90 and the ramp lag, gain falls with frequency, and the depth filter
never overshoots.

These are properties of the filter. Nothing here says the smoothed
trajectory is nearer where anyone was.

## Verified

signal with a known answer; the four directional properties; the shared
vector) and one through the CLI; the viewer's suite gains the parity replay
